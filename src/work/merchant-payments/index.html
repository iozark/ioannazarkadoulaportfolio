01

Overview

A production payment application used by merchants in everyday transactions.

The work sat inside an established product: new features, interface updates and visual changes had to fit the existing architecture.

The goal was incremental improvement — usability, consistency and maintainability — not a full redesign.

02

My Role

UX/UI design for new features and interface improvements.

I worked with stakeholders, the Project Manager and engineering to turn business requirements into solutions that could ship and stay consistent with the product over time.

03

Challenge

Years of product growth had left inconsistencies in components, interaction patterns and visual language.

A branding transition ran in parallel, so visual exploration had to stay flexible as priorities shifted.

Improvements needed to respect the existing architecture while leaving room for later change.

04

Process

Work started with shared discussions on product goals and visual direction.

Concepts explored a proposed branding update and stronger consistency across the application.

When the branding strategy changed mid-development, design shifted to reinforcing the current visual language instead of replacing it.

In parallel, feature and usability work continued with engineering and stakeholders as requirements changed.

Decisions weighed implementation effort, consistency and maintainability alongside usability.

05

Production Walkthrough

A documented payment journey from transaction initiation to successful completion.

Enter Amount

Cashiers begin the payment by entering the transaction amount using a touch-optimized keypad designed for speed and readability.

Installments

When supported, the terminal allows merchants to define the number of installments before continuing the payment flow.

Present Card

Customers can pay using contactless or chip cards while the interface clearly communicates the purchase summary.

PIN Verification

PIN entry follows familiar banking interaction patterns to support secure and intuitive authentication.

Authorization

During authorization the interface minimizes distractions while clearly communicating transaction progress.

Signature

When required, the terminal captures the customer's signature before completing the payment.

Success & Receipt

The payment concludes with confirmation and multiple receipt delivery options.

06

Outcome

Interface and feature improvements shipped into the production application, with a more consistent merchant experience.

When branding direction changed, the process showed the value of adapting design without abandoning product consistency.

07

Reflection

Product design is rarely linear.

Priorities, branding and technical limits can shift mid-project.

Useful design adapts to those shifts while keeping usability and product direction clear.

The work also sharpened how I argue for decisions that hold up for users, engineering and the business.
